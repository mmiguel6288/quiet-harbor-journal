Harlan Whitcombe, 68, founder of Whitcombe Confections (the “Snowcap” peppermint-bark people), was found dead in the locked study of his Lake Arrowhead lodge Sunday morning after his family's annual Fall Back Party, the night the clocks went back an hour.

Coroner says anaphylaxis. Harlan had a severe peanut allergy. He built a peanut-free company around it. The family wants it to be a tragic accident. I don't.

Five people at that party had a reason to want him gone. Everything Det. Okafor collected since Sunday is in this file: reports, photos, logs, receipts, phones, statements.

The D.A. won't file on a hunch. Bring me one name, and prove all three:
  MOTIVE: why they wanted him dead
  MEANS: exactly how it was done, and how they could do it
  OPPORTUNITY: when they did it, and why nobody else could have

Read carefully. Times lie. People lie more. Documents don't, but they don't explain themselves either.

You get one accusation. — D.H.
How to play
1. Open Case Files. Every file is fair game. Pin the important ones to the Evidence Board.
2. Use the board's Motive / Means / Opportunity grid to rule suspects in and out. Tap a cell: ✓ has it · ✗ provably lacks it · ? unsure.

4. Use Tools & Lab: use the Time Desk for clock math, explore the lock log, scrub the doorbell camera, walk the 3D study, crack the Evidence Locker (Harlan left his codes somewhere), and send up to 12 lab requests. Results become new exhibits.
5. Your Notes save on this device automatically.
6. Stuck? Hints come in three levels, and each one costs points.
7. When you're sure, Accuse. You must name the culprit, the motive, the means, and the opportunity, and cite evidence for each. That's one shot, then the full solution is revealed.
Played as a group? One device on the TV, phones for browsing. A printable worksheet is here. Typical solve time is 2–3 hours. Progress saves on each device.
